## Purpose and maturity
A custom rigid-body and constraint physics foundation (Godot 4.7, GDScript) intended to become a motorcycle + rider multibody simulator.
Current maturity: a generic physics foundation with ball and hinge joints, a generic one-point contact constraint with Coulomb friction, split-impulse positional stabilization, a minimal inspection scene, a generic prismatic joint with travel limits and a generic per-step force-provider hook (M2.2), the motorcycle assembly: six independent rigid bodies joined by four free hinges, a spring-damped telescopic fork and a spring-damped rear shock between chassis and swingarm (M2.1, M2.2, M2.3), an optional flat static ground with one point contact per wheel solved by the generic contact constraint (M2.4.1), and an optional simple tyre model on each wheel contact (M2.4.2: longitudinal and lateral slip, friction-ellipse combined slip, rolling resistance; illustrative prototype parameters, not measured data), which since the M2.4.2.1 correction acts only while the wheel is geometrically touching the ground and never across the air gap of a speculative contact.
Since M2.5.2 the four structural bodies (chassis, steering assembly, fork slider, swingarm) also have physical oriented-box collision geometry that touches the flat ground through eight corner contacts each (the generic contact constraint), every body has its own estimated mass (186 kg total without rider), and direct throttle, brake and steering inputs drive the rear axle, both wheel spins and the steering hinge motor. There is **no** general collision detection (only box corners against the one static plane), contact manifold, tyre relaxation/contact patch/empirical tyre model, engine, clutch, gearbox, final drive, rider or controller code, and the motorcycle has no roll stability (two narrow point contacts cannot hold it upright) and cannot ride; it falls over without a rider. There is no box-to-box or self-collision, no powertrain and no controller. See `PHASE_PLAN.md` for what is planned.

## Known limitations and technical debt (project-wide)
Measured values come from earlier agents' experiments and are REPORTED unless re-run.

### Solver and joints
- **Frozen Jacobian per step (fast rotation).** Row Jacobians are linearized once per step. Joint error grows with spin: ~`r·sin(φ)·(|ω|dt)²/2`
  (17 mm at 50 rad/s, 0.42 rad/step), independent of iteration count. A hinge free-spinning loses rate by the law `ω(t)⁻² = ω₀⁻² + f dt t` (`f = m d⊥²/I_pivot`, `PHYSICS_CONVENTIONS.md`),
  O(dt): 3.1 % of the kinetic energy in 1 s at 2 rad/s and dt = 1/120 (1.6 % at 1/240, 0.8 % at 1/480); 3 rad/s decays to 2.42 rad/s in 8 s. The loss is **unmasked since M1.3**: the former velocity-level Baumgarte term
  injected energy that offset most of it (0.15 % over the same 1 s). Mechanism: Confirmed by the derivation above and by runs with beta = 0 (kinetic-energy ratio 0.96865 with split impulses, 0.96861 with beta = 0).
- **Positional drift and its correction.** Steady-state anchor error `v²dt²/(2Lβ)`. Since M1.3 the correction is a split impulse: it never directly modifies physical linear or angular velocity, but the pose is moved without momentum,
  so total angular momentum about the origin changes by `Σ m Δx_split × v` per step (checked in the tests). In addition, the split angular correction rotates anisotropic bodies, which changes their world inertia `I_world`; with the physical `ω` unchanged, `L_spin = I_world ω` and `T_rot = ½ ωᵀ I_world ω` can therefore change (M1.3.1, tested; no compensation is applied, by design). `total_angular_momentum()` reports physical state and shows both effects.
- **Split impulses are cold started.** They are rebuilt from zero every step (required, so the correction cannot persist), so high mass-ratio configurations converge more slowly than the warm-started physical rows: a 0.1 m sphere on a 1.2 m pendulum arm peaks at 16 mm anchor error at 20 iterations and 4.0 mm at 100 (4.3 mm and 3.3 mm with the former warm-started Baumgarte bias). More iterations are the only remedy tried.
- **Slow scalar Gauss-Seidel convergence.** Large mass/inertia ratios converge slowly (0.1 m sphere on a 1 m rod: cold-start anchor error 35 mm at 20 iterations, The split pass shows the same behaviour without the warm-start benefit (previous bullet).
  11 mm at 50, 4.7 mm at 100, 3.3 mm at 200; warm starting reaches 3.3 mm at 50). The 3×3 anchor block is not solved simultaneously.
- **Hinge limit-stop convergence.** After a 3 rad/s impact on a limit (static body A) the residual separation speed is 10 % of impact speed at the default 20 iterations,
  1.5 % at 40, 0 at ≥ 80; the angle never penetrates the limit. Only the iteration count changes this (row order and β do not).
- **Hinge input domain.** Axes anti-parallel (b = −a) also satisfy the axis rows, so only joints that start satisfied are meaningful. The angle tracker needs
  < π relative rotation between samples (|rate|·dt < π; every step samples). Limits: both enabled by one flag, finite bounds only (no one-sided limit).
- **Gyroscopic update accuracy.** The implicit midpoint step conserves `|L|` and `T` of a torque-free body, but the world-frame direction of `L` has an O(dt) bounded error (0.0157 rad max at 120 Hz for the spinner), and the phase of an unstable (intermediate-axis) rotation is not reproducible against a fine-dt reference (exponential sensitivity). With applied torque the invariants no longer apply (first-order torque kick). The Newton solve fails (step aborted, fault reported) only for `|ω| dt` far outside the tested range (largest tested: 0.5 in the solver unit test, 0.05 in the scene). Float32 storage gives a ~1e-6 round-off random walk in `|L|`, `T`.
- **Numerical fault policy.** A detected fault aborts the step, restores the bodies to the pre-step state, invalidates the carried constraint impulses (cold start after recovery) and freezes the world until `clear_numerical_fault()`; `advance()` stops at the first aborted step and keeps the unprocessed time (`ARCHITECTURE.md`, D17). Detection and rollback only, no automatic recovery. The fault-detection tests inject faults inside the step (or, for static-body poses, before `step()`); cases where a fault arises organically from the solver with finite inputs were not constructed.
- **Recovery from a pose corrupted before the step.** If the caller corrupts a body's pose (the case tested: a static body's position or orientation) *before* `step()`, the start-of-step snapshot (`previous_state`) already contains the invalid pose. The check detects it before the constraint solve and aborts, but the rollback restores that same invalid pose; it cannot recover the last valid published pose. This is intentional rollback behaviour, not automatic recovery. `clear_numerical_fault()` alone does not repair the body: the next step detects the invalid pose again. The caller must restore a valid position and orientation (or remove and recreate the body) and then call `clear_numerical_fault()`.
- Joint-row accumulated impulses are only meaningful for the last step (warm-start state); after an aborted step they are zero.
- Interpretation note: after an inelastic stop a hinge can drift off the limit because the free pair of bodies legitimately tumbles (anchor forces exert axial torques). This is physics, not drift.
- Mitigations deferred (not decided): block (3×3) solve, sub-stepping, re-linearization, moving the free-rotor step after the constraint solve for exact `L` direction.

### Motorcycle assembly (M2.1, M2.2, M2.3)
- **Free hinges.** The four hinges have no limits, motor, spring or damper, the only spring-dampers are the fork's and the rear shock's, and bodies do not collide with each other: the steering and swingarm turn indefinitely and parts pass through each other. Nothing stabilizes the assembly by design (`ARCHITECTURE.md` D20). Ground contact exists only for the two wheels (see "Ground contact (M2.4.1)").
- **Suspension is a pair of linear spring-dampers.** The telescopic fork (M2.2) is one spring-damper with finite travel; the rear (M2.3) is one direct linear spring-damper between chassis and swingarm with no travel limit, bump stop, progressive rate or rocker linkage, and the swingarm hinge has no limit either. A simple tyre model exists since M2.4.2 ("Tyre model (M2.4.2)" below); the drivetrain is remaining M2 work (`PHASE_PLAN.md`). Prototype suspension values (front k = 12 kN/m, c = 400 N s/m, travel -0.12 / +0.03 m; rear k = 30 kN/m, c = 1200 N s/m, rest length = the reference attachment distance; both unloaded at the reference pose) are illustrative; there is no preload or sag computation.
- **Rear shock can collapse the simulation by design.** The rear attachment separation must stay >= 1 mm (`RearSuspension.MIN_LENGTH`): if a run drives the attachments that close (nothing limits the swingarm angle or the shock stroke), `preflight()` refuses to step and the world stays at its last state until the cause is removed; it is not clamped or repaired (`ARCHITECTURE.md` D25).
- **Rear suspension force is explicit.** Like the front one it is evaluated once per fixed step from the start-of-step state; the stability conditions on `k`, `c` and the effective mass include the lever arms about the COMs (`PHYSICS_CONVENTIONS.md`), are not checked, and for the prototype swingarm (light, long lever) `w h` is of order 0.5. For a general-geometry pair the total energy error is first order in the step (measured 0.107 of E0 over 4 s at 1/120 s for a 6 kg swingarm). The demonstration scene (default 20 solver iterations, no ground) was not examined with the rear shock active beyond the headless smoke runs.
- **Suspension force is explicit.** `FrontSuspension` is evaluated once per fixed step from the start-of-step state (symplectic Euler for the spring, explicit damping); it is stable and non-creating only for `w dt < 2` and `c dt / mu < 2` with `mu` the effective mass along the fork (`PHYSICS_CONVENTIONS.md`). Nothing checks these conditions; the prototype is at `w dt` about 0.22, `c dt/mu` about 0.2 for the two-body reduced mass, and a single step after hand-applied impulses can see ~3x larger relative velocities. The two spring seats must be collinear with the fork axis (validated), so an offset spring is not representable.
- **Prismatic joint domain.** Like the hinge, the sine-type axis/twist rows also vanish for anti-parallel axes or a twist of pi: only joints that start satisfied are meaningful. Limits: both enabled by one flag, finite bounds, no one-sided limit, no motor. The limit rows use the exact rate of the translation coordinate (lever term included); a rotating pair with axially offset COMs is not a force-free state, so its coordinate legitimately drifts (not a joint error).
- **Front chain converges slower.** The longer chain (steering -> prismatic -> slider -> hinge -> wheel) leaves a front-axle pivot-velocity residual of 4.9e-3 m/s after a 12/-9/15 N s wheel impulse at 100 iterations (6.5e-4 at 200); the M2.1 tests therefore run at 200 iterations. Constraint registration order changes this by about 3x (fork before front axle: 1.6e-3 at 100); the builder keeps the hinges-then-fork order.
- **Prototype values are illustrative.** Masses, dimensions, rake and inertias (box and hollow-cylinder primitives, formulas in `motorcycle_prototype.gd`) are plausible fixture numbers, not measured or calibrated data of any motorcycle; no result should be tuned against them. The sign conventions listed in `ARCHITECTURE.md` belong to the fixture.
- **Solver accuracy at the default settings.** `SimConfig.solver_iterations` defaults to 20; at that setting the (five-body, M2.1 measurement) chain leaves clearly visible velocity-row residuals under violent excitation (measured above), and the demonstration scene runs at that default. The M2.1 hinge tests used 100 before M2.2 and now use 200 (previous bullet). Position errors follow the frozen-Jacobian limit above (2.6e-3 rad at 6.5 rad/s relative wheel spin) and are not reduced by iterations. Higher wheel speeds than tested (> ~7 rad/s relative) were not measured.
- **Builder self-check not reachable by input.** `MotorcycleBuilder.construction_error()` is tested by displacing a built assembly; no configuration was found that makes it fail at build time (float32 pivots stay self-consistent even at 1e6 m from the origin), so its use inside `build()` is a safety net only.
- **Wheel inertia validation is orientation-only.** The configuration checks that each wheel's axle is a principal axis of its inertia, not that the moment about it matches the wheel geometry (the config stores the tensor, not the inner radius); the prototype's values are tested against the hollow-cylinder formula.

### Contacts
- **Caller-owned geometry.** The generic `ContactConstraint` keeps its anchors fixed to the bodies; the caller must re-seat them as the contact patch moves (sliding, rolling) and must create/remove contacts itself. There is no generic collision detection, manifold or persistence; a multi-point contact is several independent constraints. The motorcycle's `WheelGroundContact` does the re-seating for the wheel-plane pair (see "Ground contact (M2.4.1)").
- **Inelastic only.** No restitution; static and kinetic friction share one coefficient.
- **Speculative bias is linear.** Closing is judged with the current linear contact-point velocity; rotation of the bodies inside one step is second order (frozen Jacobian, as above).
- **Gauss-Seidel on coupled contacts.** Several contacts on one body converge iteratively (a box on four corners carries its weight to within 2 % at 20 iterations with warm starting); statically indeterminate loads are not solved exactly.
- **Friction projection cost.** The disk projection uses 60 bisection steps, only when friction is saturated (sliding).
- Contact accumulated impulses are only meaningful for the last step (warm-start state).

### Ground contact (M2.4.1)
- **Two point contacts do not give roll stability.** Each wheel touches the plane at one point (zero tyre width). The riderless assembly has no lateral support and nothing in it provides any, by design (no stand, stabilizer, damping or corrective force): any roll disturbance topples it (about 1.7 s for 0.01 rad/s, measured). The exactly symmetric undisturbed assembly stays standing only because float32 arithmetic keeps it symmetric; that is not stability. The load-transmission tests use a test-only vertical-slide fixture (`Tests/ground_rig.gd`); a complete tyre-width or rider-supported roll model is later work.
- **Only the wheels touch the ground.** There is no collision between the ground and the chassis, steering assembly, slider or swingarm, so a toppled motorcycle's body passes through the plane (measured: the chassis ends below y = 0 after the topple). A wheel lying flat (axle within about 6 degrees of the plane normal, `MIN_PLANE_PROJECTION = 0.1`) has no defined lowest point in this model; its contact is inactive.
- **Geometry approximation.** The contact point is the lowest point of a thin circle on the tyre centreline: camber shifts the real tyre's contact toward the shoulder (up to about `w/2 sin(camber)`) and this is ignored; no tyre section radius, no patch. A wheel without a tyre specification uses the generic isotropic Coulomb disk with one coefficient for static and kinetic friction; a wheel with a tyre uses the M2.4.2 model instead ("Tyre model (M2.4.2)" below).
- **Static-ground only.** The plane is flat and static; the ground body keeps an identity orientation at the plane point. No slopes, terrain, moving or deformable ground.
- **Contact sampled at the start of the step.** The wheel point, the gap and the normal are evaluated from the start-of-step pose and frozen for the step like every other row (frozen Jacobian); a wheel moving fast tangentially or rotating inside one step is judged by the linear speculative bias. Activation beyond `activation_distance` (prototype 5 cm) plus the approach distance of one step only discards carried impulses; it never adds force.
- **Prototype values.** The ground friction (0.8), the reference pose (the rear wheel touches the plane, the front is 3 mm above it) and the 5 cm activation distance are illustrative fixture numbers.
- **Not examined.** The scene was only run headless; graphics, camera and markers were not looked at. Sustained behaviour above 60 s of simulated time was run only in the headless motorcycle scene (150 s, no fault) and for 60 s in the two scripted experiments (not committed as tests).

### Finite-width tyre geometry (M2.5.1)
- **Geometry only.** The contact, the normal response and the tyre forces still use the zero-width one-point geometry of M2.4.1/M2.4.2; the finite-width point is not fed to the solver, so the documented ground-contact approximation (camber contact ignored) still holds for the simulation. No contact patch, no multiple contacts, no distributed load (M2.5.2 and later, not started).
- **Simple profile.** Symmetric circular crown with a flat parameter set; no sidewall below the shoulder, no deformation, no measured or calibrated profile. The width, crown radius and the admissible bounds (width <= 0.5 m, crown radius <= 10 m, shoulder drop <= half the wheel radius) are model limits chosen for validity and overflow safety, not tyre data. No prototype configuration sets a geometry (default null).
- **One candidate point.** `lowest_point` reports the single lowest point; a camber beyond the shoulder clamps it to the shoulder (`at_shoulder`), where the true tyre sidewall would carry the contact. Operating domain: axle at least about 6 degrees from the plane normal (`MIN_PLANE_PROJECTION = 0.1`).
- **Float32 pose.** Poses are float32; the brute-force oracle of the tests resolves the minimum to about 1e-5 m. Coordinates of order 1e9 m are only guaranteed finite, not accurate.
- **Geometry only.** The tyre profile is drawn by the wireframe (G) again; the wheel-ground contacts still do not use it.

### Tyre model (M2.4.2)
- **Illustrative parameters, not tyre data.** Stiffness 15 (longitudinal) and 10 (lateral), grip scale 1.0 on the surface friction 0.8, rolling resistance 0.015 with speed scale 0.2 m/s, low-speed reference 0.5 m/s and rolling radius equal to the wheel radius are fixture values; nothing was calibrated or tuned against a result, and no mass, suspension or wheel geometry was changed.
- **Simple model.** No relaxation length or tyre deformation (the force follows the slip instantly), no camber thrust, no self-aligning moment, no load sensitivity, no contact patch or width, one point per wheel, no temperature or wear. The friction ellipse is the stated envelope, not a validated tyre characteristic.
- **No static friction.** At rest a tangential load makes the wheel creep: the force is proportional to the sliding speed up to `3 mu v_ref / k` (0.08 m/s for the prototype). The low-speed reference regularizes the zero-speed singularity; it is also what makes the slip finite.
- **Direction of a saturated force.** It follows the stiffness-weighted slip `(k_x sigma_x/mu_x, k_y sigma_y/mu_y)`; it equals the sliding direction (isotropic Coulomb) only for equal normalised stiffness. The prototype has `k_x != k_y`, so a locked wheel sliding diagonally is not pushed exactly against its velocity.
- **Frozen per step.** The tyre axes, slip rows, reference speed and the spin used by rolling resistance are those of the start of the solve (like every row); the slip itself and the normal load are re-evaluated each solver iteration. The factor `phi` is lagged one iteration (monotone, contraction < 1/3). Rolling resistance is explicit in the spin.
- **No tyre action on the landing step.** The tyre acts only while the start-of-step gap is <= 0.1 mm (`WheelGroundContact.TOUCH_TOLERANCE`); a wheel that lands from a speculative gap (0.1 mm up to the activation distance plus the approach distance) has no tyre force, no rolling resistance and no reported load on that one step, and acts from the next step on, cold. The gap is judged from the start-of-step pose like every row, so a wheel that closes a larger gap inside one step is treated as separated for that step. The 0.1 mm tolerance is an illustrative fixture value (a resting wheel stays within about 1e-5 m of the plane).
- **Rolling resistance is a moment** on the wheel about its axle, outside the slip ellipse, and only for a rolling wheel; a locked sliding wheel has none.
- **Gyroscopic coupling.** A lateral force at the contact point rolls the wheel; with the wheel's spin this precesses it and shows as a small longitudinal force or angular-impulse difference (measured about 3 % of the impulse in the momentum test). Physical, not a tyre defect.
- **Cornering and combined scenarios are not demonstrable on the free assembly.** The riderless motorcycle has no roll stability, so lateral and combined braking + lateral cases are exercised with a single wheel and with the TEST-ONLY planar fixture of `Tests/tire_rig.gd` (forward and vertical translation free, lateral translation and chassis rotation removed, so no load transfer or pitch). Since M2.5.2 the scene offers continuous throttle, brake and steering keys instead of the former toggles.
- **External test inputs are not a drivetrain.** Drive and brake in the tests and in the scene are axle torques applied to a wheel body; nothing models an engine, clutch, gearbox, final drive, brake hydraulics, ABS or a rider.
- **Not examined.** Graphical appearance of the HUD lines was never looked at (the old `T` / `B` toggles were replaced in M2.5.2). Behaviour above about 20 s of simulated driving was run only in scripted, uncommitted experiments.

### Collision boxes, masses and controls (M2.5.2)
- **Box-ground only.** Box corners against the one static plane; no box-to-box, no self-collision, no general collision detection, no contact manifold or edge/face reduction (all corners within the activation distance are kept; at most 8 per box, deterministic by corner index). Penetrating-corner response is the generic contact's (split impulse).
- **Masses are estimates** (middleweight naked bike, no rider, 186 kg), box inertia is that of a uniform solid box of the collision dimensions; no calibration.
- **Controls are direct inputs**, not a powertrain, brake system or rider: no engine state, gearbox, ABS, PID, countersteering or balance. With zero steering input the steering motor stays on with target rate 0 (a rate lock up to its torque cap, not free steering). Brake torque is capped at half the one-step stopping torque of the reduced inertia (documented margin); throttle and brake superpose without priority. Without a rider the motorcycle falls over; the fall is only exercised by a short scripted test, not studied.
- **Wheels** keep the tyre contact as their only ground contact; a toppled machine rests on its structural boxes with the wheels as before.
- **Not examined:** graphical appearance of the new wireframe, the HUD lines and the keyboard path through a real window; long falls and sliding of boxes were run only in short tests.

### Tyre surface sampling (M2.5.3 Stage A)
- **Geometry only.** `TirePatchSampler.sample()` produces the candidate surface samples of one tyre. Nothing uses them yet: the wheels still carry the one-point M2.4.1 contact, the tyre model is unchanged, and no normal support, patch state or debug output exists.
- **Quadrature accuracy.** Area weights come from a trapezoid rule over the meridian angle (second order; measured area error 1.1e-4 relative on the test tyre at 33 lateral nodes, halving the node spacing divides it by about 4) times a periodic rule over the circumference (spectral). The weights are the basis of Stage B and are only checked for convergence here.
- **Bounds.** At most 64 lateral and 256 circumferential nodes per tyre (at most 16384 samples); the lateral count must be at least 2 so that both shoulders are sampled.
- **Degenerate orientations** (axle parallel to the plane normal) produce valid samples; only the reference `TireGeometry.lowest_point()` is undefined there.

### Numerics and validation
- Godot `Vector3`/`Quaternion`/`Basis` are float32 (≈1e-7 relative error; ~8e-6 m resolution at 100 m). Double-precision Godot build is the deferred migration path (`PHYSICS_CONVENTIONS.md`).
- Inertia validation does not enforce the triangle inequality of principal moments and rejects tensors with reciprocal condition number < 1e-5.
- Invalid configuration, mass properties or constraints are refused (no stepping), not repaired; the caller must check `step()`'s return value or `SimWorld.last_error`.

### Verification gaps
- The test suite takes about 170 s (about 3x the pre-M2.2 time), mostly six-body runs at 200 iterations; no CI exists.
- Visual inspection so far: only the superseded M2.5.1.2 wireframe design, from software-rendered screenshots (none for M2.5.2); the rest of the presentation/debug code and scenes have no automated tests.
- A GDScript runtime error aborts the whole test run (engine limitation); treat as failure.

## Important unresolved architectural issues
- Solver strategy for stiff, fast or high-mass-ratio systems (block solve vs sub-stepping vs re-linearization) is undecided; it matters for fast-spinning bodies such as the M2.1 wheels (measured only up to ~7 rad/s relative spin), where the frozen-Jacobian error above grows.
- Float32 vs double-precision build is undecided.
