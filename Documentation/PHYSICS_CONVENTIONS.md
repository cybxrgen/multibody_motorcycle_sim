# Physics Conventions

**Authoritative for:** units, coordinate frames, quaternion/angular-velocity, mass and inertia, force/impulse, constraint/Jacobian, hinge-joint, prismatic-joint, front-suspension spring-damper,
contact and friction, stabilization, time-stepping and numerical-precision conventions. Binding for all simulation code.
**Belongs elsewhere:** implementation progress and known limitations → `PROJECT_STATE.md`; module design → `ARCHITECTURE.md`;
test results and methodology → `TESTING.md`.
**Update when:** a convention, equation, sign rule or numerical assumption changes. Conventions that depend on Godot behaviour are checked by `Tests/test_conventions.gd`.

## Units and coordinates
- SI: m, kg, s, N, N·m, N·s (impulse), kg·m² (inertia).
- Right-handed, +X right, +Y up, local **-Z forward** (`Vector3.FORWARD`). X × Y = Z.
- Positive rotation about an axis is counter-clockwise looking down the axis toward the origin (right-hand rule).
- Default gravity (0, -9.81, 0) m/s², configurable in `SimConfig`.

## State
- Body position is the **center of mass (COM) in world coordinates**. It is the sole integration reference.
  Visual/mesh origins that differ from the COM are a presentation concern.
- Orientation: unit `Quaternion` rotating **local → world** (`Basis(q)` is the local→world matrix R).
  The body setter re-normalizes on every assignment.
- Linear velocity: velocity of the COM, world frame.
- Angular velocity ω: **world frame**, rad/s.
- Quaternion product `a * b` is the Hamilton product: apply `b` first, then `a`. A world-frame rotation increment
  `dq` is applied as `q' = dq * q`.

## Inertia
- `local_inertia` is a 3×3 `Basis` about the COM, in the body frame. Stored as a full symmetric matrix
  (diagonal principal tensors are just a special case) so full tensors can be used later.
- `I_world = R · I_local · Rᵀ`, `I_world⁻¹ = R · I_local⁻¹ · Rᵀ`. Recomputed whenever orientation changes.
- Static body: mass 0, inverse mass 0, inverse inertia = zero matrix. A **finite** mass ≤ 0 means static (the inertia argument is
  ignored). A non-finite mass is invalid, never static.
- **Validity of a dynamic body's inertia** (`SimMassProperties.validate_inertia`): the tensor must be (1) finite,
  (2) symmetric, (3) positive definite, (4) well conditioned. A positive determinant alone proves none of this
  (e.g. `diag(-1,-1,1)`). Method: normalize by the largest |element| (verdict independent of physical scale), all arithmetic in
  float64; symmetry within 1e-5 of the largest element (~84 float32 ulps: allows R·D·Rᵀ rounding); Cholesky with strictly
  positive pivots; reciprocal 1-norm condition number ≥ 1e-5 (below that fewer than ~2 digits of the float32 inverse survive).
  Slender bodies are fine (rod r=1 cm, L=1 m has rcond ≈ 6e-4); the triangle inequality of principal moments is not enforced.
- Invalid mass properties are observable (`valid == false`, `error_message`, `push_error`), carry zero inverse mass/inertia, and are
  refused by `SimWorld.add_body` (returns null) and `SimRigidBody.set_mass_properties` (returns false); `SimWorld.step()` also
  refuses a world containing one. The inverse inertia is computed in float64 and stored as float32 (`Basis.inverse()` overflows
  float32 for tensors above ~1e12 although the true inverse is representable).
- Box: I = m/12·diag(y²+z², x²+z², x²+y²) with full side lengths. Sphere: 2/5·m·r².

## Forces, torques, impulses
- Force (N) and torque (N·m) accumulate over a step and are consumed (then cleared) by that step.
- Force at a world point p: force += F, torque += (p − x_com) × F.
- Impulse (N·s) changes velocity immediately: Δv = J/m, Δω = I_world⁻¹ ((p − x_com) × J). Impulses never touch the accumulators.
- Angular impulse: Δω = I_world⁻¹ L.
- State-dependent forces (springs, dampers) are not applied by the caller once per frame: they are registered as force providers and evaluated once per FIXED step, after gravity and before the velocity integration, from the state at the start of that step (`ARCHITECTURE.md`, D21).

## Rotational dynamics
Continuous equation: `I_w ω̇ = τ − ω × (I_w ω)`, i.e. `α = I_w⁻¹ (τ − ω × I_w ω)`; the gyroscopic term is included. Equivalently the world angular momentum
`L = I_w ω` obeys `L̇ = τ`. In the body frame (`R` = local→world, `I` = local inertia, `ω_b = Rᵀω`, `L_b = I ω_b = RᵀL`):
`L̇_b = L_b × I⁻¹ L_b + τ_b` (Euler's equations). Torque-free motion has two quadratic invariants, `|L_b|² = |L|²` and `T = ½ L_b·I⁻¹ L_b`, and since
`|L|²/(2 I_max) ≤ T ≤ |L|²/(2 I_min)` the angular speed of a torque-free body stays in `[|L|/I_max, |L|/I_min]` forever.

**Discrete update (`SimIntegrator.integrate_velocities`), per step, current orientation `R`:**
1. Torque kick (world frame, as before): `ω* = ω + dt I_w⁻¹ τ`.
2. Gyroscopic update, implicit midpoint on Euler's equations in the body frame: `L_b = I Rᵀ ω*`, then solve
   `L' = L_b + dt · Lm × (I⁻¹ Lm)`, `Lm = (L_b + L')/2`, by Newton iteration in float64 (3×3 Jacobian `J = (dt/2)([Lm]ₓ I⁻¹ − [I⁻¹ Lm]ₓ) − 1`, start `L' = L_b`,
   stop when the Newton step is below `1e-12 |L_b|`, at most 24 iterations; typically 3–5).
3. `ω' = ω* + R I⁻¹ (L' − L_b)`: only the increment is transformed to the world frame, so a body whose `L_b` does not change (zero spin, principal-axis spin,
   isotropic body) keeps its angular velocity bit for bit.
4. Orientation (`integrate_positions`, unchanged): `q' = exp(dt (ω' + ω_split)/2) q`, normalized.

**Numerical properties.**
- The midpoint rule conserves every quadratic invariant of the flow exactly. `(Lm × y)·Lm = 0` gives `|L'|² − |L_b|² = 2 dt (Lm × I⁻¹Lm)·Lm = 0`, and for symmetric `I⁻¹`
  `T' − T = dt (Lm × I⁻¹Lm)·I⁻¹Lm = 0`. Hence `|L|` and `T` of a torque-free body are conserved to the Newton tolerance and the float32 storage of the result,
  **independent of dt**, and `|ω|` cannot run away (bound above). Solve inputs are not clamped and nothing is damped or renormalized.
- Not conserved: the world-frame **direction** of `L`. The orientation is advanced with the end-of-step `ω'` (semi-implicit order, needed so that constraint
  impulses act between the two phases), not with the midpoint angular velocity, so the world-frame precession of `L` errs by `O(dt)` per unit time, bounded in
  measurements (`PROJECT_STATE.md`). The torque kick is first order (kick, then free rotation: Lie–Trotter splitting); with a torque the invariants above no longer apply.
- Accuracy is first order overall. The trajectory of an unstable (intermediate-axis) rotation is exponentially sensitive to rounding and to dt; only the invariants and
  the bounded range of `|ω|` are meaningful over long runs, not the phase of the tumbling.
- Failure modes: non-finite input, a singular Newton matrix or non-convergence (only for `|ω| dt` far outside the regime of interest) make
  `integrate_velocities` return false without touching the angular velocity; `SimWorld` then aborts the step (`ARCHITECTURE.md`, "Validation and failure behavior").
- Rejected: the former **explicit** update `ω += dt I_w⁻¹(τ − ω × I_w ω)`. Its error in `T` is `+½ dt² c·I⁻¹c ≥ 0` per step (`c = ω × I_w ω`) and the same sign holds for
  `|L|²`: both grow monotonically, faster as `|ω|` grows, until the iteration overflows (observed for the inspection-scene spinner after ~86 s at 120 Hz). A single
  linearized implicit-Euler step (Newton once on the implicit Euler equations) was also prototyped: stable but dissipative (it removes `T` and `|L|` at `O(dt)` instead of
  conserving them), so it would damp the tumbling it is meant to show. A fully consistent rotation update (Cayley/Lie-splitting with the mid-step angular velocity, exact
  `L` direction) needs the free-rotor step to move into the position phase, after the constraint solve; deferred as a larger change to the step structure.

## Constraint Jacobian conventions
- A row is `J = [Jv_a, Jw_a, Jv_b, Jw_b]` (world frame). Constraint velocity `Cdot = Jv_a·v_a + Jw_a·ω_a + Jv_b·v_b + Jw_b·ω_b`.
- A scalar impulse λ applies `Δv_a = m_a⁻¹ Jv_a λ`, `Δω_a = I_a⁻¹ Jw_a λ` (same for b). Impulses are therefore along +Jᵀ.
- Effective mass `1/(K + γ)` with `K = J M⁻¹ Jᵀ`, softness γ ≥ 0.
- Row solve: `λ = −(Cdot + bias + γ·λ_acc)/(K+γ)`, then `λ_acc` is clamped to `[min, max]` and only the clamped delta is applied.
  Bilateral rows use (−∞, +∞); unilateral (contact-like) rows use `min = 0`.
- Split row solve (positional correction, see "Stabilization: split impulses"): the same `J` and effective mass act on the **split velocities** `v_s, w_s`:
  `lambda_s = -(J.v_s + b_s + gamma*lambda_s_acc)/(K+gamma)`, `lambda_s_acc` clamped to the row's split bounds, only the clamped delta is applied to `v_s, w_s` (never to `v, w`).
  `b_s` is the split bias; `lambda_s_acc` starts at 0 every step (no warm start).
- Ball joint: `C = (x_b + r_b) − (x_a + r_a)`, with `r = R · local_anchor`. Rows along world X, Y, Z:
  `Jv_a = −e, Jw_a = −(r_a × e), Jv_b = +e, Jw_b = r_b × e`. Physical bias 0 (`Cdot = 0`); split bias `b_s = (beta/dt) C.e` (bilateral).

## Hinge joint
- Frame data per body, local: anchor, unit axis, unit reference direction perpendicular to the axis. Zero angle <=> `R_a*ref_a == R_b*ref_b`. `HingeJointConstraint.from_world(a, b, anchor, axis, reference)` (`hinge_joint_constraint.gd`) makes the current pose the zero angle. Input is validated, never repaired: unit length / perpendicularity within 1e-6 (8 float32 ulps); valid input is then re-normalized exactly.
- World frame (A): `a = R_a*axis_a`, `p = R_a*ref_a`, `q = a x p`; `b = R_b*axis_b`.
- Axis alignment rows: `C_p = p.b`, `C_q = q.b` (sine-type angle error, zero for b = +-a). `Jw_a = p x b`, `Jw_b = b x p` (and q), physical `bias = 0`, split `b_s = (beta/dt) C` (bilateral). Perpendicular to the axis, so rotation about it is free.
- Angle: `atan2(w.q, w.p)`, `w = R_b*ref_b`; positive right-handed about +a (B turns counter-clockwise seen from +a relative to A). Continuous: only wrapped differences (`wrapf(d, -PI, PI)`) are accumulated; valid while the relative rotation between two samples is < PI (every step samples). A tilt of b changes it at second order.
- Hinge-axis rows: `Jw_b = +a, Jw_a = -a` (`Cdot = (w_b - w_a).a`, the angle rate); the upper limit uses the negated pair.
- Limits (unilateral, impulse in [0, +inf)): lower `C = angle - lower`, upper `C = upper - angle`. Physical bias `C/dt` while `C >= 0` (speculative: engages only if the limit would be crossed this step, not a correction) and `0` when violated (the angle may not move further out: `Cdot >= 0`); continuous at 0. A violated limit is corrected by the split channel only: split bias `(beta/dt) C` (negative), split bounds `[0, +inf)`; the split row is inactive while `C >= 0`. Lower and upper must be finite, `lower <= upper`; both enabled together.
- Motor: row bias `-target_speed`; positive speed => positive angle rate (B along +a, A along -a); impulse bound `+-max_torque*dt` (`dt = config.fixed_timestep`); `max_torque = 0` or disabled => bounds [0,0]. Target is first clamped into the rate interval the enabled limit rows admit (`[-bias_lower, +bias_upper]`, the **physical** limit biases: a violated limit contributes 0), so motor and limits never push against each other and the motor never drives further into a violated limit. The motor row has no split channel.
- Momentum: all rows except the anchor rows are pure equal-and-opposite angular impulses; anchor rows change total angular momentum about the origin by `C x lambda`. The split pose correction adds the term described in "Stabilization: split impulses".

## Prismatic joint (`PrismaticJointConstraint`)
Generic slider: one free relative translation along the joint axis, five constrained DOF (2 lateral translations, 3 rotations), optional finite travel limits. Full row layout in the header of `prismatic_joint_constraint.gd`.
- **Frame data per body, local:** anchor (a point of the axis line), unit axis, unit reference direction perpendicular to the axis (fixes the twist). `PrismaticJointConstraint.from_world(a, b, anchor, axis, reference)` makes the current pose the reference configuration (both anchors are the same world point, aligned axes, zero twist, translation 0). Input is validated, never repaired (unit length / perpendicularity within 1e-6; valid input is re-normalized exactly; limits finite, `lower <= upper` when enabled; bodies different, not both static).
- **World frame (A):** `a = R_a*axis_a`, `p = R_a*ref_a`, `q = a x p`; `b = R_b*axis_b`, `w = R_b*ref_b`; `d = (x_b + r_b) - (x_a + r_a)`.
- **Translation coordinate** `s = d.a` (m): signed displacement of B's anchor from A's anchor along A's axis; positive along `+a`; 0 at the reference configuration. Rate `s_dot = a.d_dot + d.(w_a x a)` (the axis turns with A), `translation_rate()`.
- **Lateral rows (2):** `C = d.e`, `e in {p, q}` (the anchor offset perpendicular to the axis, in A's moving frame). `Jv_a = -e`, `Jw_a = -((r_a + d) x e)`, `Jv_b = +e`, `Jw_b = r_b x e`. The `d x e` part of `Jw_a` is the effect of A turning the frame while the anchors are axially apart; it is not zero for `s != 0` (checked against central finite differences). Physical bias 0, split bias `(beta/dt) C` (bilateral). `Jv.a = 0`: the rows never act along the axis.
- **Axis rows (2) and twist row (1):** `C = p.b`, `C = q.b` as for the hinge; `C_t = w.q` (sine of the twist about the axis), `Jw_b = w x q (= +a)`, `Jw_a = q x w (= -a)`, `Jv = 0`; physical bias 0, split `(beta/dt) C`. Together the three rotational rows fix all three relative rotations. Like the hinge, anti-parallel axes or a twist of pi also satisfy the sine-type rows, so only joints that start satisfied are meaningful.
- **Limits (unilateral, impulse in [0, +inf)):** lower `C = s - lower`, row `Jv_b = +a, Jw_b = r_b x a, Jv_a = -a, Jw_a = -((r_a + d) x a)` (`Cdot = s_dot`); upper `C = upper - s`, the negated row. Physical bias `C/dt` while `C >= 0` (speculative: engages only if the coordinate would cross the limit within this step, not a correction) and 0 when violated (`Cdot >= 0`: no further outward motion); a violated limit is corrected by the split channel only (`(beta/dt) C`, negative, bounds `[0, +inf)`), so no physical velocity is injected and no kinetic energy is created. Disabled limits: bounds [0, 0], the axis is free, and no row has an axial component.
- **Impulse signs:** `lower_limit_impulse() >= 0` acts along `+a` on B (and `-a` on A); `upper_limit_impulse() >= 0` acts along `-a` on B (and `+a` on A).
- **Momentum:** all rows have `Jv_a = -Jv_b`; the constraint never changes a body's COM velocity along the axis, so with equal and opposite axial impulses the relative axial COM velocity changes by exactly `J (1/m_a + 1/m_b)` (test identity). The split pose correction adds the terms described in "Stabilization: split impulses". Rows are linearized once per step (frozen Jacobian).

## Front suspension spring-damper (`FrontSuspension`)
A linear spring and linear viscous damper along the axis of the fork's prismatic joint, between body A (steering assembly) and body B (fork slider).
- **Axis and coordinate.** `u` = the joint's world axis (`world_axis_a()`), pointing from the steering assembly toward the slider (down the fork), so a positive displacement is **extension**. Spring seats `p_a` (fixed point of A) and `p_b` (fixed point of B), configured as world points at the reference pose; they must lie on one line parallel to `u` (validated, not repaired). `l = (p_b - p_a).u`, `l0` = its value at the reference pose, **displacement** `x = l - l0` (m); for a satisfied joint `x` equals the prismatic coordinate `s`. **Rest displacement** `x_rest` (m, explicit): the spring is unloaded at `x = x_rest`. **Relative velocity** `v = u.(v(p_b) - v(p_a))`, `v(p) = v_com + w x (p - x_com)` (m/s; positive while extending); it is the rate conjugate to the force pair, so the damping power is exactly `-c v^2`.
- **Law.** `F = -k (x - x_rest) - c v`, `k >= 0` N/m, `c >= 0` N s/m (finite; zero allowed), `F` in N. **Sign convention:** `F` is the scalar force on B along `+u` and on A along `-u`: force on B `= +F u` at `p_b`, force on A `= -F u` at `p_a`. Restoring: `x > x_rest` gives `F < 0` (B is pulled back toward A, compressing), `x < x_rest` gives `F > 0`; the damping force `-c v u` opposes the relative axial motion in both directions.
- **Torque.** The forces are applied AT the seats (`apply_force_at_point`), so each body also receives `tau = (p - x_com) x F` about its COM (non-zero because the COMs are off the line of action). With the seats collinear along `u` the pair is central: net force 0 and net torque about every point 0, so total linear and angular momentum are unchanged by the suspension.
- **Directions.** The force has no component perpendicular to `u`; it never acts along the five directions the prismatic joint constrains (the joint's rows resist the motion the off-COM torque causes).
- **Timing.** Evaluated once per fixed step from the start-of-step state by `SimWorld` (force provider), never at the render rate.
- **Energy.** Spring energy `U = 1/2 k (x - x_rest)^2` (J); damping power `-c v^2 <= 0` (W). The provider never edits poses or velocities. Discretely (a single oscillator, mass `mu`, `w = sqrt(k/mu)`, `h = dt`) the update `v' = v - h (k (x - x_rest) + c v)/mu`, `x' = x + h v'` is symplectic Euler with explicit damping: for `c = 0` the quantity `v^2 + w^2 x^2 - w^2 h x v` is conserved exactly, so for a release from rest the energy stays within `E0/(1 + w h/2) <= E <= E0/(1 - w h/2)` (bounded oscillation, no secular growth, no creation, for `w h < 2`); explicit damping dissipates for `c h/mu < 2`. The frequency of the discrete oscillator is shifted by about `(w h)^2/24`. These are time-step conditions on `k` and `c` (the prototype: `w h` about 0.22, `c h/mu` about 0.2); the code neither checks nor corrects them. In a coupled assembly `mu` is an effective mass that can be much smaller than the two-body value, and a first step that starts from artificially injected, constraint-inconsistent velocities sees larger relative velocities than a settled system (observed ~3x on the first damping step of the prototype after hand-applied impulses).

## Rear suspension spring-damper (`RearSuspension`)
A linear spring and linear viscous damper acting **directly** between the chassis and the swingarm along the instantaneous line joining one attachment point fixed in each body. No joint, constraint or extra body represents the shock.
- **Geometry.** `p_c`, `p_s` = world attachment points (fixed points of the chassis and of the swingarm, configured as world points at the reference pose; they need not lie on any body axis). `d = p_s - p_c`, `l = |d|`, `n = d / l`, directed **from the chassis attachment toward the swingarm attachment**; recomputed from the current poses at every step, so it follows both bodies and is never assumed vertical or parallel to a body axis. `l0` = the configured **rest length** (m, explicit, `> 0`, independent of the reference pose).
- **Rate.** `v(p) = v_com + w x (p - x_com)` is the velocity of a body-fixed point (the rotational contribution of **both** bodies is included); `l_dot = n . (v(p_s) - v(p_c))` (m/s, positive while the shock lengthens). A rigid motion of the pair leaves `l` unchanged and gives `l_dot = 0` exactly.
- **Law and sign convention.** `F = -k (l - l0) - c l_dot`, `k >= 0` N/m, `c >= 0` N s/m (finite; zero allowed), `F` in N. Force on the swingarm `F_s = +F n` at `p_s`, on the chassis `F_c = -F n` at `p_c`. Restoring: `l > l0` gives `F < 0` (the swingarm is pulled toward the chassis, the shock shortens), `l < l0` gives `F > 0`; the damping part `-c l_dot` opposes the change of length in both directions.
- **Torque and momentum.** The forces are applied AT the attachment points (`apply_force_at_point`), so each body also receives `tau = (p - x_com) x F` about its own COM. The pair is equal and opposite and collinear (both lie on the line `p_c p_s`): net force 0 and net torque about every point 0, so an isolated pair conserves total linear and angular momentum. A static body receives nothing (like gravity); the other body still receives the full force.
- **Energy.** `U = 1/2 k (l - l0)^2` (J). Power of the pair `F_s . v(p_s) + F_c . v(p_c) = F l_dot`; the damping part is `-c l_dot^2 <= 0` and the spring part is `-dU/dt`. The provider only adds forces; it never edits poses or velocities. It is evaluated once per fixed step from the start-of-step state (symplectic Euler for the spring, explicit damping), so the same time-step conditions as for the front suspension apply (`w h < 2`, `c h/mu < 2`, with an effective `w`, `mu` that include the lever arms: `w^2 = k (1/m_c + 1/m_s + |r_c x n|^2/I_c + |r_s x n|^2/I_s)` for isotropic bodies, `r` the attachment lever). A light swingarm with a long lever makes the shock stiff in rotation (the prototype is at `w h` of order 0.5); the code neither checks nor corrects this. For general geometry (rotation, off-COM seats) the total energy error of the first-order scheme is O(h), not bounded by the single-oscillator formula.
- **Degenerate geometry.** `n` is undefined as `l -> 0`. Pose arithmetic in float32 (`local = R^-1 (P - x)`, `world = x + R local`) carries about 2e-7 m for metre-sized coordinates, so the direction has an error of about `2e-7 / l` rad; the threshold `MIN_LENGTH = 1 mm` (about 5000x the rounding; direction error <= 2e-4 rad) is the smallest accepted separation. A separation below it, or a non-finite one (finite coordinates can overflow the float32 length, and `+INF >= MIN_LENGTH` holds), configured or reached during a run, makes `validate()` report an error, so `add_force_provider()` and the world's `preflight()` refuse it and the world stops stepping with its state unchanged; nothing is clamped or repaired. Called directly, `direction()` and `apply_forces()` produce zero (never a non-finite value).
- **No preload, bump stop or linkage.** `l0` is a free parameter (the prototype sets it to the reference length, so the shock is unloaded at the reference pose); there is no travel limit on the shock, no progressive rate and no rocker linkage.

## Contact (`ContactConstraint`)
One-point contact between two bodies; the caller supplies the geometry and creates, refreshes and removes the constraint (no generic collision detection exists; the wheel-plane geometry of the motorcycle is in "Ground plane and wheel contact").
Several contact points are several independent constraints.
- **Data.** Anchor on each body (local frame), unit normal `n` in body A's local frame, friction coefficient `mu >= 0`, optional `tangent_rotation`. World: `pa = x_a + R_a*anchor_a`, `pb = x_b + R_b*anchor_b`, `n = R_a*normal_a`. Validated, never repaired: finite anchors, finite nonzero normal of unit length within 1e-6 (valid input is then normalized exactly), finite `mu >= 0`, finite `tangent_rotation`, two different bodies, not both static.
- **Sign convention.** `n` points from A toward B. Gap `g = n.(pb - pa)`: `g > 0` separated, `g < 0` penetrating. Normal relative velocity `vn = n.(v_b(p) - v_a(p))`: `vn > 0` separating, `vn < 0` approaching.
- **Shared contact point.** `p = (pa + pb)/2`. All rows use lever arms `r_a = p - x_a`, `r_b = p - x_b`, so the equal-and-opposite impulses act at one point (exact conservation of linear momentum and of angular momentum about any origin, up to float32). A tangential offset between `pa` and `pb` is not constrained.
- **Caller duty.** Anchors are fixed to the bodies. When the contact patch moves relative to a body (sliding, rolling) the caller must re-seat the anchors, otherwise lever arms and gap drift away from the real contact. The motorcycle's `WheelGroundContact` fulfils this duty every fixed step.
- **Normal row.** `Jv_a = -n, Jw_a = -(r_a x n), Jv_b = +n, Jw_b = r_b x n`, so `Cdot = vn`. Impulse bounds `[0, +inf)`: it can push, never pull. The row solve enforces `Cdot + bias >= 0`, i.e. `vn >= -bias`.
  - Speculative, `g >= 0`: require the gap not to become negative, `g + vn*dt >= 0`  =>  `bias = g/dt`. Inactive (zero impulse) unless the bodies would cross within this step. Closing is judged with the current velocities (frozen Jacobian, linear in `vn`). Physical channel; the split row is inactive.
  - Penetrating, `g < 0`: the **physical** normal row has `bias = 0` (`vn >= 0`: no further approach, never an attractive impulse; continuous with the speculative bias at `g = 0`). The penetration itself is removed by the **split** normal row: split bias `(beta/dt) g` (negative, `beta = SimConfig.baumgarte_factor`; no separate parameter), bounds `[0, +inf)`, i.e. `vn_split >= beta|g|/dt`, so the pose moves by `beta|g|` per step (`g + vn_split*dt = (1 - beta) g`) and no velocity is added to either body. Same form as the hinge limits (`C = g`), with the sign fixed by the convention above.
  - No restitution: contacts are perfectly inelastic in the normal direction.
- **Friction (isotropic Coulomb).** Tangent rows `t1, t2` have the same Jacobian pattern with `t` instead of `n` and bias 0 (the tangential relative velocity at `p` is driven to zero). With accumulated tangent impulses `lambda_t = (l1, l2)` and normal impulse `lambda_n`: admissible set `|lambda_t| <= mu * lambda_n` (a disk, not a square). Per solver iteration, in this order:
  1. normal row (clamped to `[0, inf)`);
  2. block update `y = lambda_t - K^-1 c`, `K_ij = J_i M^-1 J_j^T` (2x2, current inertias), `c = (Cdot_t1, Cdot_t2)`;
  3. `lambda_t <- argmin_{|x| <= mu*lambda_n} (x - y)^T K (x - y)` (K-metric projection; `y` if already inside; zero if `mu*lambda_n = 0`);
  4. apply only `lambda_t_new - lambda_t_old`.
  Friction is physical and reads only the physical normal row's accumulated impulse; the split normal impulse is a different field and never enters the disk radius, the warm start or any friction quantity.
  Because step 3 minimizes the same quadratic as the unconstrained block update, it is the exact block Gauss-Seidel step for the friction subproblem; at a converged sliding contact the friction impulse is antiparallel to the remaining slip (maximum dissipation). A radial (Euclidean) scaling would not have this property when `K` is anisotropic and is therefore not used. Sticking = `y` inside the disk; sliding = boundary active. Static and kinetic friction share one `mu`.
  Projection: `x(nu) = (K + nu I)^-1 K y` with `|x(nu)|` strictly decreasing in `nu >= 0`; `nu` is found by 60 bisection steps (float64) and the end of the bracket inside the disk is used, so the bound is never exceeded.
- **Tangent basis.** `e` = world axis with the smallest `|n.e|`; `t1 = normalize(n x e)`, `t2 = n x t1` (right-handed with `n`), then both rotated about `n` by `tangent_rotation`. The block solve and the disk are rotation invariant, so results do not depend on the basis; it can be rebuilt every step.
- **Warm starting.** Normal impulse: like every row (scaled by `warm_start_factor`, clamped to `[0, inf)`). Friction: stored between steps as a world vector (the basis changes with `n`), scaled by `warm_start_factor`, projected into the current tangent plane, then radially clipped to the disk of radius `mu * (warm-started lambda_n)` before it is applied; so the carried impulse is admissible for the current normal impulse and `mu`. Without warm starting both start at zero.
- **Momentum.** Every row has `Jv_a = -Jv_b`; the lever arms share `p`. Total (physical) linear momentum is conserved, and the physical impulses conserve angular momentum about the origin up to float32 rounding (spheres in free flight; non-spherical bodies additionally change the direction of their spin angular momentum through the gyroscopic update, see "Rotational dynamics"). A split correction moves the bodies without changing their physical linear or angular velocity (and hence not `p = m v`); it can still change quantities computed from the corrected pose, see "Stabilization: split impulses".
- **Numerical assumptions.** Rows use the Jacobians linearized at the start of the step (see the frozen-Jacobian limitation in `PROJECT_STATE.md`). Tests of a single-row solve use `1e-5` for float32 quantities of order 1.

## Ground plane and wheel contact (M2.4.1)
- **Plane.** `{ x : n . (x - p0) = 0 }` with `p0` a world point on the plane and `n` the unit normal pointing AWAY from the ground into free space (the prototype: `p0 = 0`, `n = +Y`). Signed distance `s(x) = n . (x - p0)`: `s > 0` above the ground, `s = 0` on it, `s < 0` below it. The ground body is a static body at `p0` with identity orientation; it has no velocity and cannot gain any.
- **Wheel.** Axle `u = q * (1, 0, 0)` (body-local X, the wheel's principal axis; `q` the current orientation). The wheel is approximated by a thin circle of radius `R` on the tyre centreline, centred on the COM, in the plane perpendicular to `u`. The lowest point relative to `n` is `p_w = x - R d` with `d = (n - (n.u) u) / |n - (n.u) u|` (the direction of `n` inside the wheel plane); `|n - (n.u) u| = sin(angle(u, n))`. The matching plane point is `p_g = p_w - s(p_w) n`; the signed gap is `g = s(p_w)`.
- **Analytic checks.** Upright: `p_w = x - R n`, `g = n.x - R - n.p0`. Camber `phi` about the longitudinal axis (axle `(cos phi, sin phi, 0)`, `n = +Y`): `p_w = x + (R sin phi, -R cos phi, 0)`, height `x_y - R cos phi`. Spin by `theta` about the axle: the world point does not move, the body-frame anchor is `R_x(-theta)(0, -R, 0) = (0, -R cos theta, R sin theta)`: the contact moves around the rim, rolling at `w` moves it at `w`.
- **Normal orientation.** Body A is the ground, body B the wheel, so the contact normal (A toward B) is `+n` and the `ContactConstraint` gap `n.(p_b - p_a)` equals `g` (`p_b - p_a = g n` because `p_g` is the projection of `p_w`). The shared contact point is the midpoint; the two anchors differ only along `n`, so no tangential offset is left unconstrained.
- **Local frames.** `local_anchor_a = q_a^-1 (p_g - x_a)`, `local_anchor_b = q_b^-1 (p_w - x_b)`, `local_normal_a = q_a^-1 n`, re-derived at every fixed step from the start-of-step pose.
- **Valid domain and degeneracy.** The thin-circle model needs `|n - (n.u) u| >= MIN_PLANE_PROJECTION = 0.1` (axle within about 84 degrees of the plane, camber up to about 84 degrees from upright). Below it the lowest point is undefined (a flat wheel) and the contact is inactive; the vector is never normalised when it vanishes. Non-finite inputs are rejected the same way. Valid float32 geometry has about 1e-7 relative rounding per operation.
- **Approximations.** Zero tyre width and no tyre section radius in the CONTACT (the finite-width geometry of "Finite-width tyre geometry (M2.5.1)" exists but is not used by the contact): under camber the true lowest point is on the shoulder, up to about `w/2 sin(phi)` away, and this is ignored. One point per wheel, no patch, no manifold, no tyre deformation, no relaxation. A wheel WITHOUT a tyre specification uses the generic isotropic Coulomb disk (one `mu` for static and kinetic friction); a wheel with a tyre uses the tyre model ("Tyre behaviour (M2.4.2)") instead, never both. Rolling without slip emerges from the contact constraint alone: a wheel rolling at `v` has `w = -v/R` about +X for forward (-Z) motion; a sliding wheel with no spin ends in rolling at `v0 / (1 + k)`, `k = I_axle / (m R^2)` (`m ds/dt = -mu m g`, `I dw/dt = mu m g R`).
- **Activation.** The contact is active while `g <= activation_distance + max(0, -v_n) dt` (`v_n` the normal velocity of the wheel point relative to the plane); this only decides whether carried impulses are kept, because the generic speculative row (`vn >= -g/dt`) already produces no impulse for a wheel that cannot reach the plane within the step.
- **Roll.** Two such point contacts leave the riderless motorcycle without any lateral support. The inverted-pendulum growth rate of the disturbance is of order `sqrt(g/h)` per second (`h` the centre-of-mass height), so tilting by 30 degrees from a 0.01 rad/s disturbance takes about 1.7 s in the prototype.

## Tyre behaviour (M2.4.2)
A simple, documented tyre model acts through the wheel-ground contact of a wheel that has a tyre specification. It is a fixture model: the stiffness, grip, rolling-resistance and low-speed values of the prototype are illustrative, not measured tyre data, and nothing was tuned against a result.
- **Tyre axes (SAE-like: x forward, y right, z down).** `n` the plane normal (up), `u = q (1,0,0)` the wheel axle. Longitudinal axis `e_x = (n x u) / |n x u|` (the rolling direction projected on the ground; forward = -Z for the upright prototype), lateral axis `e_y = e_x x n = (u - n (n.u)) / |u - n (n.u)|` (the axle projected on the ground, pointing to the wheel's right). Both lie in the ground plane, are orthogonal and are exact for a tilted wheel; they exist while `|n x u| >= MIN_PLANE_PROJECTION` (the same domain as the contact geometry).
- **Contact-point velocity.** `v_c = v + w x (p_c - x)` with `v`, `w` the COM velocity and angular velocity of the wheel, `x` its COM and `p_c` the contact point; the ground is static, so `v_c` is the velocity relative to the ground. Identities used by the solver rows (checked in the tests): `r x e_x = R u` and therefore `v_c.e_x = v.e_x + R (w.u)` for any tilt (only the axle component of `w` moves the contact point longitudinally), and `v_c.e_y = v.e_y - R sin(angle(u,n)) (w.e_x)` (a roll rate moves it laterally). `r = p_c - x`.
- **Slip.** Spin `Omega = -(w.u)` (positive for forward rolling: a wheel rolling toward -Z turns about -X). Hub speed `v_h = v.e_x`. Slip velocities (tread relative to the ground) `s_x = -(v_c.e_x) + (R_eff - R) Omega`, `s_y = -(v_c.e_y)`; reference speed `D = sqrt(v_h^2 + v_ref^2)`; slip `sigma_x = s_x / D`, `sigma_y = s_y / D` (dimensionless, finite at zero speed because `v_ref > 0`). `R` is the contact geometry radius, `R_eff` the tyre's effective rolling radius (free rolling is `R_eff Omega = v_h`, zero slip).
- **Sign conventions.** `sigma_x > 0` DRIVING: the tread is faster than the ground and the force on the wheel is forward (+`e_x`). `sigma_x < 0` BRAKING: the force is backward; a locked wheel at forward speed `v` has `sigma_x = -v/D` (`-1` at high speed, never below). Rolling backward reverses `v_h` and the tread speed together; a backward-rolling wheel turning too slowly has positive slip. `sigma_y > 0`: the contact point slides to the wheel's LEFT, the restoring force `F_y` points RIGHT (+`e_y`); a wheel whose hub moves right while rolling forward has `sigma_y < 0` and a force to the left. The force on the wheel is `+(k N phi) sigma`, so it always opposes the sliding of the contact point. Forces are applied to the wheel at the contact point; the ground takes nothing.
- **Force law (friction ellipse).** Peak coefficients `mu_x = grip_x mu_s`, `mu_y = grip_y mu_s` (`mu_s` the surface friction of the ground specification). With `e_x' = k_x sigma_x / mu_x`, `e_y' = k_y sigma_y / mu_y`, `rho = |(e_x', e_y')|`, `g(rho) = rho - rho^2/3 + rho^3/27` for `rho < 3` and `1` for `rho >= 3`, and `phi(rho) = g(rho)/rho` (`phi(0) = 1`): `F_x = phi k_x N sigma_x`, `F_y = phi k_y N sigma_y`. Then `(F_x/(mu_x N))^2 + (F_y/(mu_y N))^2 = g(rho)^2 <= 1` for every slip: ONE common factor shares the traction budget; the axes are never clamped independently. Small slip: slope `k_x N`, `k_y N`. Pure slip saturates at `mu N` (at `rho = 3`, i.e. `sigma = 3 mu / k`). Braking reduces the lateral force at fixed `sigma_y` and lateral slip reduces the traction at fixed `sigma_x` monotonically (`phi` decreases with `rho`).
- **Normal load.** `N = lambda_n / dt` where `lambda_n` is the accumulated physical normal impulse of the SAME solver iteration (units: N s / s = N). It is zero for an inactive contact (all impulses discarded), never negative, and never a stale value from a previous step; it is not a constant.
- **Geometric contact versus speculative contact.** The generic normal row is speculative: while the wheel has a small positive gap `g` (up to the activation distance) and approaches, it may carry a positive impulse that merely prevents penetration this step (`vn >= -g/dt`). That impulse is collision prevention, not tyre load, and traction cannot act across an air gap. The tyre therefore acts only while the wheel is geometrically touching, `g <= TOUCH_TOLERANCE` (0.1 mm, `WheelGroundContact`, penetration included; `g` is the start-of-step gap the normal row uses). While the contact is active but `g > TOUCH_TOLERANCE`: the normal row, its split correction and its warm start are the generic ones (unchanged); the tyre has no warm start, no tangential impulse and no rolling resistance, its carried tangential and rolling-resistance state is cleared, and `normal_load()`, the force limits and the utilisation report zero (the impulse is available as `speculative_normal_impulse()`). Consequence: on the step in which a wheel lands from a speculative gap the tyre does not act; it acts from the next step on, cold.
- **Implicit tangential solve.** Per iteration, after the normal row: the current slip `s` (including all impulses applied so far) gives `phi`; the tangential impulse `lambda = (lambda_x, lambda_y)` solves `lambda = A sigma(lambda)`, `A = diag(phi k_x lambda_n, phi k_y lambda_n)` (this is `dt N phi k`), with `sigma(lambda) = (s + G (lambda - lambda_cur)) / D` and `G = S M^-1 P^T` the exact 2x2 slip response of the wheel (`S` the slip Jacobian rows, `P` the impulse rows at the contact point, `M^-1` the inverse mass/inertia). Closed form `(I - A G / D) lambda = A (s - G lambda_cur) / D` (`G` has negative diagonal: an impulse reduces the slip), then a radial scaling into the ellipse. `phi` is lagged one iteration: the map `phi -> phi'` is monotone with slope `<= (rho/3) a/(1+a) < 1/3` (`a` the implicit gain), so the lag converges at least geometrically. An explicit tyre force is unstable here: `c R^2 dt / I` with `c = k N / D` exceeds 2 for the prototype wheel at low speed.
- **Rolling resistance.** A separate effect, a MOMENT about the axle opposing the spin: `M = c_rr N R_eff psi(R_eff Omega / v_rr)`, `psi(x) = x / sqrt(1 + x^2)`, applied as an angular impulse `M dt` from the same load `N`, with the spin frozen at the start of the solve. Proportional to the normal load, zero at zero spin (smooth, odd), zero without contact, outside the slip ellipse (it is not a traction force). The equivalent tread force is `-M/R_eff`. As a contact FORCE it would be cancelled by the slip force of a free wheel and would not slow it down; a free rolling wheel loses speed at `c_rr g psi / (1 + k)`, `k = I_axle/(m R^2)` (tested).
- **Friction ownership.** The tyre model is the only owner of the tangential response of a wheel with a tyre: the generic Coulomb block of `ContactConstraint` is bypassed (never warm started, never solved), the two tangent rows carry only the tyre impulse, and `mu_s` is the surface coefficient, never a second disk. Disabling the tyre (`tire_forces_enabled = false`) gives a frictionless normal contact.
- **Units.** SI: `sigma` dimensionless, `k` dimensionless (force per unit normal load per unit slip), `N`, `F` newtons, `M` N m, `Omega` rad/s, `v_ref`, `v_rr` m/s, impulses N s (`lambda = F dt`).
- **Approximations (limits of the model).** No relaxation length or tyre deformation (slip force responds instantly, regularized only through `v_ref`); at rest a wheel has no static friction, it creeps under a tangential load (force proportional to the sliding speed up to `3 mu v_ref / k`); the direction of a saturated force follows the stiffness-weighted slip direction `(k_x sigma_x/mu_x, k_y sigma_y/mu_y)`, which equals the sliding direction only when `k_x/mu_x = k_y/mu_y` (isotropic Coulomb sliding is reproduced for equal normalised stiffness, tested); `D`, the Jacobians and the rolling-resistance factor are frozen at the start of the solve like every row; a wheel's own roll/precession couples a lateral force into a small longitudinal one; zero tyre width, one point, no camber thrust, no self-aligning moment, no load sensitivity, no temperature or wear; one friction coefficient for static and kinetic friction.

## Finite-width tyre geometry (M2.5.1)
Pure surface geometry of a tyre of finite width; it does not enter the contact solver yet (the wheel contact is still the one-point model of "Ground plane and wheel contact").
- **Frames.** Wheel frame: origin at the wheel COM, axle = local +X (`u = q (1,0,0)` in world). A surface point is addressed by the lateral offset `s` (m, along the axle from the centreline, `+` toward local +X, `s in [-w/2, +w/2]`) and by the unit direction `e` of its radius, perpendicular to `u`. World point `P = x + s u + r(s) e`; wheel-frame coordinates `q^-1 (P - x)` have `X = s` and `|(Y,Z)| = r(s)`.
- **Profile.** Circular crown of radius `rho` (`crown_radius`, `rho >= w/2`) tangent to the centreline radius `R` (the wheel radius of the contact geometry) at `s = 0`: `r(s) = R - rho + sqrt(rho^2 - s^2)`. Symmetric: `r(s) = r(-s)`; `r(0) = R`; shoulder drop `r(0) - r(w/2) = rho - sqrt(rho^2 - w^2/4)`; `rho = w/2` is a semicircle, large `rho` a nearly flat tread. Admissible: `0 < w <= 0.5 m`, `w/2 <= rho <= 10 m`, shoulder drop `<= R/2` (these bounds also keep `rho^2` far from overflow).
- **Lowest point against a plane** (unit normal `n` pointing away from the ground, point `p0`). With `a = n.u`, `L = |n - a u| = sqrt(1 - a^2)` and `h_c = n.(x - p0)`: the height of `(s, e)` is `h = h_c + a s + r(s) (n.e)`. For fixed `s` it is minimal at `e = -(n - a u)/L` (the same direction as the thin-circle model), giving `h(s) = h_c + a s - L r(s)`, which is convex in `s`. `h'(s) = 0` gives `s* = -rho a` exactly (independent of `R` and `L`); the lowest point has `s = clamp(s*, -w/2, +w/2)`, and a clamped point lies on a shoulder (`at_shoulder`). Signed gap `g = h(s)`; plane point `p_g = P - g n`.
- **Camber.** For `n = +Y` and axle `(cos gamma, sin gamma, 0)`: `a = sin gamma`, `s* = -rho sin gamma`, `e = (sin gamma, -cos gamma, 0)`, `g = y_c + s sin gamma - r(s) cos gamma`. The contact moves toward the LOWER end of the axle by `rho sin gamma` (until it reaches the shoulder), i.e. it migrates across the tread instead of staying on the centreline; reversing the camber of a symmetric tyre mirrors the result (`s -> -s`, same gap). Upright (`a = 0`): `s = 0` and the point equals the thin-circle point `x - R n`. The finite-width lowest point is never above the thin-circle point.
- **Spin.** Rotation about the axle does not change `u`, `s*`, `e` or the world point; only the wheel-frame coordinates `(Y, Z)` rotate.
- **Degeneracy and validity.** `L >= MIN_PLANE_PROJECTION = 0.1` (the same limit as the one-point contact, equal by test); below it the lowest point is undefined and the result is invalid. Also invalid: invalid tyre dimensions or wheel radius, non-finite pose or plane data, a quaternion or normal whose length differs from 1 by more than 1e-5, and results that overflow float32 (finite pose values whose differences overflow).
- **Limitations.** Symmetric circular crown only; no sidewall below the shoulder (a wheel leaned beyond the shoulder is not described); no tyre deformation, no contact patch, no measured or calibrated profile; the tyre width is independent of the wheel's visual and inertia width. The result is one candidate point, not a patch.

## Box-ground contact and basic controls (M2.5.2)
- **Collision box:** owner body frame (origin = COM); centre `c`, orientation `Rb`, full size `s`, half extents `h = s/2`. Corner `k`: `p_k = x + R (c + Rb (sx hx, sy hy, sz hz))`, `sx = +1` if bit 0 of `k` is set else `-1` (`sy` bit 1, `sz` bit 2). Gap `g = n . (p_k - plane_point)`, ground point `p_k - g n`. Normal from the ground (A) toward the box body (B) is the plane normal; the contact equations are those of `ContactConstraint` above. The COM must lie inside the box.
- **Mass and inertia:** every body has its own mass; for the four box bodies `I = m/12 diag(b^2 + c^2, a^2 + c^2, a^2 + b^2)` for the full sizes `(a, b, c)` of its collision box (uniform solid box about the COM, box centred on the COM in the prototype); wheels keep the hollow-cylinder inertia with the axle along local X. Estimated prototype masses (middleweight naked bike without rider and fuel): 128 / 14 / 8 / 9 / 12 / 15 kg for chassis / steering / slider / swingarm / front / rear wheel, 186 kg; estimates, not data.
- **Throttle:** `tau = throttle * T_max` about the rear axle `u` (wheel local +X); forward rolling is a NEGATIVE rate about `+u`, so the torque on the wheel is `-tau u`, the reaction on the swingarm `+tau u`. No force is applied by the control.
- **Brake:** `w_rel = (w_wheel - w_carrier) . u`, `I_red = 1/(1/I_wheel,u + 1/I_carrier,u)`; `T = min(brake T_max,wheel, 0.5 I_red |w_rel| / dt)`; torque `-sign(w_rel) T u` on the wheel and the opposite on the carrier (power `-T |w_rel| <= 0`). Zero at `w_rel = 0`.
- **Steering:** the hinge motor target rate is `steering * rate_max` with `steering` in `[-1, 1]`; positive steering = positive steering-hinge angle = steering to the LEFT (A / Left key = +1). Torque cap `motor_max_torque`; the motor is clamped by the hinge's own limit rows. Zero input keeps the motor on with target rate 0.

## Stabilization: split impulses
Position error (constraint drift, contact penetration, a violated hinge limit) is corrected through a **separate velocity channel**, so the split solve never directly modifies physical linear velocity, physical angular velocity or the physical impulse accumulators. Diagnostics that are functions of the pose as well as of the velocity (spin angular momentum, rotational kinetic energy of an anisotropic body, angular momentum about the origin) can change indirectly because the pose changes; see the consequences below.
- **Two velocity channels per body and fixed step.** Physical `v, w` (gravity, forces, motor, contact normal impulse, friction, joint velocity constraints, speculative biases) and split `v_s, w_s` (positional correction only). `v_s, w_s` are zero at the start of every step (`SimWorld` zeroes them before the solver and the solver zeroes those of the bodies it touches before the split pass), are written only by the split pass, are used only by the position integration, and are zero again right after it. They are never stored as body velocity and never accumulate across steps. Momentum, kinetic and potential energy, `velocity_at_point`, motor rate, friction limits and contact velocity use `v, w` only. Static bodies have zero inverse mass and inertia, so their split velocities stay exactly zero.
- **Split solve.** Same rows, Jacobians and effective masses (`K = J M^-1 J^T`, current world inverse inertia) as the physical solve, applied to `v_s, w_s`: `lambda_s = -(J.v_s + b_s + gamma*lambda_s_acc)/(K+gamma)`, clamped to the row's split bounds, with its own accumulated impulse. It runs after the physical pass, for `solver_iterations` iterations over all active constraints. It is **never warm started**: `lambda_s_acc` is reset in every `prepare`. The two passes do not read each other's state, so their order only fixes determinism.
- **Biases.** With `beta = SimConfig.baumgarte_factor` (the only stabilization parameter; no other tuning value exists) the split bias of a positional row is `b_s = (beta/dt) C`, so the converged split solve satisfies `J.v_s = -(beta/dt) C` and the pose moves by `-beta C` per step: a fraction beta of the error is removed. The physical bias of the same row is 0 (a joint row enforces `Cdot = 0`).
- **Which effects use which channel.**

  | Effect | Channel |
  |---|---|
  | Ball anchor, hinge anchor, hinge axis alignment error | split (`b_s = (beta/dt) C`, bilateral); physical `Cdot = 0` |
  | Violated hinge limit (`C < 0`) | split (`b_s = (beta/dt) C`, bounds `[0, +inf)`); physical bias 0 |
  | Penetrating contact (`g < 0`) | split (`b_s = (beta/dt) g`, bounds `[0, +inf)`); physical bias 0 |
  | Speculative contact (`g >= 0`), speculative hinge limit (`C >= 0`) | physical (`bias = C/dt`); split row inactive |
  | Prismatic lateral, axis-alignment and twist error | split (`b_s = (beta/dt) C`, bilateral); physical `Cdot = 0` |
  | Violated prismatic limit (`C < 0`) | split (`b_s = (beta/dt) C`, bounds `[0, +inf)`); physical bias 0 |
  | Speculative prismatic limit (`C >= 0`) | physical (`bias = C/dt`); split row inactive |
  | Hinge motor, contact friction, contact normal impulse against approach, gravity, forces | physical only |

- **Position integration.** `x' = x + dt (v + v_s)`, `q' = exp(dt (w + w_s)/2) q` (world-frame increment applied as `dq * q`, then normalized, exactly as before; a body with `v_s = w_s = 0` reproduces the previous update bit for bit). Nothing is teleported and no anchor is overwritten.
- **Consequences.**
  - *No kinetic energy through the velocity channel.* The split solve injects no physical velocity: a body at rest with constraint errors keeps `v = w = 0` exactly, so its kinetic energy stays exactly zero (for a moving anisotropic body see the orientation-dependent bullet below); its pose converges to the constraint manifold. For a single exactly solved row the error shrinks as `(1 - beta)^n`. The former velocity-level bias `(beta/dt) C` left `v = beta|C|/dt` in the body (0.48 m/s for a 2 cm contact penetration at the default beta and 120 Hz).
  - *Momentum.* The two split impulses of a constraint are equal and opposite, so they do not move the centre of mass of a dynamic pair, but they move `x` without changing `p`: total angular momentum about the origin changes by `sum m_i dx_i,split x v_i` per step (plus the physical `C x lambda` of joint anchor rows). Linear momentum is unaffected. `SimWorld.total_angular_momentum()` reports physical state and therefore shows this term.
  - *Orientation-dependent quantities (anisotropic bodies).* The split angular velocity `w_s` rotates the body, and a rotation changes the world inertia `I_w = R I R^T` of a body whose principal moments differ. The physical `w` is unchanged, so the spin angular momentum `L_spin = I_w w` and the rotational kinetic energy `T_rot = 1/2 w^T I_w w` are evaluated with the corrected `I_w` and can change. Example (tested): `w` along a principal axis of moment `I_1` and a split rotation by angle `phi` about a perpendicular principal axis of moment `I_2` give `T_rot = 1/2 |w|^2 (I_1 cos^2 phi + I_2 sin^2 phi)`, i.e. a change of `1/2 |w|^2 (I_2 - I_1) sin^2 phi` at fixed `w`. Rotating the body changes `I_w`, so `L_spin = I_w w` need not remain parallel to `w`: in this example it is parallel to `w` before the correction (`w` lies on a principal axis) and in general not after it, so the direction of `L_spin` changes although `w` does not. This is an expected consequence of the pose correction, not a leak of split velocity into physical velocity: for an isotropic body (`I_w` independent of orientation) `L_spin` and `T_rot` are unchanged. The solver does not compensate for it (no momentum or energy correction term is applied, by design). It is not claimed that the quantities change for every correction (a rotation about the principal axis that `w` points along leaves them unchanged), only that they are not invariant in general. Together with the translational term above, the change of `L` about the origin during a split correction is `sum m_i dx_i,split x v_i` plus the change of `sum I_w,i w_i` caused by the rotated `I_w,i`.
  - *Frozen-Jacobian dissipation is no longer masked.* Rows are linearized once per step. Free hinge rotation about an offset COM leaves a tangential error `delta^2/2` (`delta = w dt`) after each centripetal impulse, so the rate obeys `dw/dt = -f w^3 dt/2`, i.e. `w(t)^-2 = w0^-2 + f dt t`, with `f = m d_perp^2 / I_pivot` (`d_perp`: COM distance from the axis). The former Baumgarte velocity added energy that happened to offset this loss; without it (beta = 0, or with split impulses) the loss is visible. It is `O(dt)` per unit time.
  - *Cold-started convergence.* Split impulses are not warm started, so high mass-ratio configurations need more iterations for the same correction accuracy than the warm-started physical rows (tracked in `PROJECT_STATE.md`).
- **Purpose and limits.** Distinguish: **numerical error** (integration order), **constraint drift** (anchor/axis separation, bounded by the split correction), **physical motion** (swing). beta is the fraction of error removed per step, range `[0, 1]` (beta > 1 would over-correct); there is no hidden damping. First-order drift remains: the steady-state anchor error scales with `v^2 dt^2/(2 L beta)`.

## Tyre surface sampling (M2.5.3 Stage A)
- **Parameterisation.** Axle `u = q X`, radial frame `b1 = q Y`, `b2 = q Z`, radial direction `e(theta) = cos(theta) b1 + sin(theta) b2`. Each meridian is the circular crown of radius `rho` with centre at radial distance `R - rho`; with the meridian angle `phi`, `s = rho sin(phi)` and `r = R - rho + rho cos(phi)` (identical to `TireGeometry.profile_radius`). Surface point `x(phi, theta) = p + u s + e r`.
- **Normal.** Outward unit normal `n = cos(phi) e + sin(phi) u` (equal to `(sqrt(rho^2 - s^2) e + s u) / rho`); finite at the shoulders, including the semicircular limit `rho = w/2`.
- **Area element.** `dA = rho r dphi dtheta` exactly (`|x_phi x x_theta| = rho r`).
- **Quadrature.** Trapezoid in `phi` over `[-asin(w/2rho), asin(w/2rho)]` with half weights at the two shoulder nodes (both shoulders are included); uniform periodic rule in `theta = 2 pi j / N` (spectral for the periodic integrand). Sample weight `rho r(phi_i) dphi_i dtheta` in m^2. Sum of weights → exact tread area `2 pi rho (2 (R - rho) asin(w/2rho) + w)` as the grid is refined; the trapezoid error is second order in the lateral spacing.
- **Gap.** `gap = n_plane . (x - p0)` (positive above the plane). Not a force; the normal response is Stage B.
- **Rolling.** Spinning by `alpha` about the axle maps the circumferential index: `theta` of the spun sample is the unspun angle `theta + alpha` (rotation about +X takes the radial Y toward Z).

## Tyre patch normal support (M2.5.3 Stage B)
Normal support (`TirePatchNormalSupport`). The tyre response built on it is documented in "Tyre patch force integration (M2.5.3 Stage C)" below; a patch built without a tyre has no tangential force, slip or rolling resistance. Implementation: `ARCHITECTURE.md`.
- **Formulation.** Area-density Kelvin-Voigt (spring-damper) support over the Stage A samples. Per sample `i` (area `A_i`, m^2): gap `g_i = n . (x_i - p0)`, compression `delta_i = -g_i`, normal velocity `vn_i = n . (v_wheel(x_i) - v_ground(x_i))` (> 0 separating). `k_i = k_A A_i`, `c_i = c_A A_i`, sample force `F_i = max(0, k_i delta_i - c_i vn_i)` along the ground normal `n`, applied at `x_i`. Aggregate load `N = sum F_i`; moment about `q`: `sum (x_i - q) x n F_i`.
- **Parameters.** `k_A` (`stiffness_density`): N/m^3 (= Pa/m, pressure per metre of compression), valid in (0, 1e10]. `c_A` (`damping_density`): N s/m^3 (= Pa s/m), valid in [0, 1e9]. `max_compression` (m): hard-stop depth, in (0, R/2]. They are new parameters; the tyre-force stiffnesses, friction and rolling resistance are neither reused nor reinterpreted. Test values (`k_A = 4e6`, `c_A = 1e5`, 5 cm) are not tyre data.
- **Area weighting.** `sum A_i k_A delta_i -> k_A integral(delta dA)` as the sampling is refined, so sample density never multiplies the support. The weights are tread (surface) area, not projected footprint area: `k_A` is a stiffness per unit tread area and the force is taken along the ground normal (approximation).
- **Solver form.** Backward Euler per sample: `lambda = dt F(g + dt vn')`, i.e. the existing soft row `Cdot + bias + softness lambda = 0` with `softness = 1/(dt (c_i + dt k_i))`, `bias = k_i g/(c_i + dt k_i)`, bound `lambda in [0, inf)`. The sample load is `lambda/dt` (N); the aggregate load is the sum of the active rows' `lambda/dt`. Unconditionally stable. The look-ahead adds numerical damping `dt k_i`: the effective damping is `c_A + dt k_A` per unit area (3.3e4 N s/m^3 for `k_A = 4e6`, `dt = 1/120`).
- **Unilateral and excess penetration.** Per-sample impulse `>= 0`: the ground never pulls (a fast-separating tyre receives exactly no load). A sample compressed by more than `max_compression` at the start of the step becomes a rigid non-penetration row (`softness = 0`, `bias = 0`, `vn' >= 0`) with a split-impulse correction toward `max_compression` (pose only). The hard stop is physical load and is not continuous with the spring at that depth. Penetration is bounded by `max_compression` plus one step of approach (`|vn| dt`), because samples are classified from the start-of-step gap.
- **Speculative versus physical.** There is no speculative row: a compliant sample needs none, and one would stop the tyre at zero compression. Only samples with `g < 0` have a row, impulse or load; positive-gap approach produces no response and no load (M2.4.2.1 lifecycle preserved).
- **Lifecycle.** Samples are re-derived each step from the start-of-step pose and are indexed (lateral, circumferential); the index follows a material point of the tyre, so a row whose sample stays compressed keeps its impulse as warm start and a row whose sample left the ground is dropped with it. Complete separation, an invalid sampling or a non-finite pose clears all rows, impulses and load; re-contact starts cold (identical to a fresh patch). Non-finite carried impulses are discarded.
- **Velocity-side and impulse-side Jacobians.** The row has two angular Jacobians for the wheel, deliberately different (`VelocityJacobianRow`, a `ConstraintRow` subclass; the linear part `J_lin = n` is shared). *Impulse side (physical wrench):* a normal impulse `lambda_i n` at the sample position `x_i` (`r_i = x_i - p`, `p` the wheel centre) is the wrench (force `n lambda_i`, moment `r_i x n lambda_i`), so `J_imp_ang = r_i x n` with nothing removed; warm start and every solve apply `dv_B = M^-1 n lambda`, `domega_B = I_w^-1 (r_i x n) lambda`. The angular impulse the solver applies is therefore the moment of the normal impulses at their actual sample positions, and `applied_wheel_moment()` (installed impulse `J_ang` times load) and `total_moment_world(p)` (sample positions times load) are the same sum. The component about the axle `(r_i x n) . u` is the fore-aft offset of the line of action times the load: zero for the quasi-static footprint (fore-aft symmetric), non-zero for any fore-aft asymmetric load. *Velocity side (compression rate and effective mass):* the tread is a surface of revolution about the axle `u`; the velocity a spin induces is tangent to it and is not a rate of compression. Left in `vn_i` it loads the leading samples more than the trailing ones: probe (full Jacobian for everything, 17x96, 2 cm, 30 and 60 rad/s) load +27 % and +76 % (`c_A = 0`), +185 % and +412 % (`c_A = 1e5`), moment about the axle 13 % to 20 % of `N R`. The row therefore measures and weighs the compression with `J_vel_ang = (r_i x n) - u (u . (r_i x n))`: `vn_i = n . v + J_vel_ang . omega` (evaluated from the current velocities in every iteration) and `K_i = 1/m + J_vel_ang . I_w^-1 J_vel_ang`, effective mass `1/(K_i + gamma_i)`. `J_vel_ang . u = 0`, so a change of the axle spin never enters `vn_i` or `K_i`, at preparation or in later sequential iterations. Because `u` is a principal axis of the wheel inertia (an enforced precondition, next bullet), the axle component of the wrench does not leak into off-axle angular velocity through `I_w^-1`; the exact response of `Cdot` to a unit impulse is `J_vel M^-1 J_imp^T`, which equals the used `J_vel M^-1 J_vel^T` under that precondition. The Jacobians and `u` are those of the start of the step (frozen-Jacobian limitation of the project); velocities are live. Bottomed rows use the same velocity map. *Split correction:* a pose correction, not a physical impulse, uses the velocity-side Jacobian to measure and to apply (the gradient of the gap has no axle component), so it never spins the wheel about its axle; the physical wrench never enters the split velocities. Rolling resistance and traction belong to Stage C.
- **Precondition: the wheel axle is a principal inertia axis (enforced).** The used effective mass `J_vel M^-1 J_vel^T` differs from the exact response `J_vel M^-1 J_imp^T` by `J_vel_ang . I_w^-1 u (u . (r_i x n))`, which is zero for every sample if and only if `I_w^-1 u` is parallel to `u`, i.e. `u` is a principal axis (`I u = lambda u`). `SimMassProperties` accepts full tensors, so a valid wheel need not satisfy it, and `TirePatchNormalSupport.validate()` rejects a wheel that does not: the body-frame tensor must not turn the axle away from itself, measured by the scale-independent residual `rho = |I u - u (u^T I u)| / |I u| <= 1e-4` with `u = (1, 0, 0)`. With the symmetrised products of inertia `J_ab = (I_ab + I_ba)/2` (as `SimMassProperties` symmetrises its stored inverse) `I u = (I_xx, J_xy, J_xz)` and `rho = sqrt(J_xy^2 + J_xz^2) / sqrt(I_xx^2 + J_xy^2 + J_xz^2)`, the sine of the angle between `I u` and `u`; it is relative to the AXIAL row, not to the largest tensor element, and it does not change when the tensor is multiplied by a constant. *Frame:* the BODY frame, where the axle is exactly `(1, 0, 0)` and the tensor is the stored one. The verdict does not depend on the world orientation (`I_w u = R I e_x`, so the property is the same in every pose); a world-frame test would add the float32 rounding of `R I R^T`, would reject a rotated principal wheel (non-diagonal `I_w`) and would accept a non-principal wheel posed so that `I_w` is diagonal. *Why this residual:* write `I u = I_xx u + c` with `c` perpendicular to `u` and `|c| = rho |I u|`; then `I^-1 u = (u - I^-1 c) / I_xx`, so the error term above is `-J_vel_ang . (I^-1 c) (u . (r_i x n)) / I_xx` and its size relative to the effective-mass term is of the order of `rho` for any ratio of axial to transverse moments. The tolerance that this replaced (coupling against 1e-5 of the LARGEST tensor element) bounds `|c|` by a transverse moment, so for a small axial moment `|c| / I_xx` could reach 1e-5 times the transverse-to-axial ratio (a wheel with `I = diag(1, 1000, 1000.5)` and `I_xy = 0.004`, `I_xz = -0.003` was accepted at `rho = 5e-3`). *Tolerance:* `rho <= 1e-4`, the same residual and value as the axle check of `MotorcycleConfig` (so a configured wheel is no longer accepted by the configuration and refused by the patch). Float32 level: the rounding in `I_xy`, `I_xz` of a tensor assembled through general rotations is about 1.5e-7 times the largest TRANSVERSE moment (it follows the largest element, not `I_xx`), measured as `rho` of at most 2.7e-7 for an axial moment that is the largest or comparable (axial : transverse 1 : 1 to 10000 : 1; 126 assembled tensors per ratio), 1.6e-6 at 1 : 10, 1.3e-5 at 1 : 100 and 1.5e-4 at 1 : 1000 (axial : transverse). The tolerance is therefore about 370 times above the roundoff of an ordinary wheel and accepts assembled tensors down to an axial moment of roughly 1/600 of the transverse moments; a wheel more anisotropic than that has an axle that float32 cannot tell from a non-principal one and must be given as a diagonal body-frame tensor. A tolerated residual changes the effective mass by a relative amount of the order of `rho`: in an exploratory float64 experiment (not committed; random sample geometry, axial : transverse moments from 1000 : 1 : 1 to 1 : 1000 : 1000) it was at most 4.4e-4 at `rho = 1e-4`. *Unusable tensors:* the components are divided by their largest magnitude before the norms are taken (no overflow, underflow or scale dependence); a non-finite entry, `I u = 0` or `I_xx <= 0` gives no residual (`nan` in the message) and is REPORTED, never accepted; a valid dynamic body never reaches these branches.  *Consequences:* the check is part of the ordinary `validate()`, so registration refuses an invalid wheel and `SimWorld.preflight()` refuses every step while the wheel's mass properties violate it (also when they are replaced after registration); the error message states the requirement, the measured axial moment and products of inertia, the residual and the tolerance. The wheel inertia is never projected, diagonalised or repaired, and the support is never silently skipped. A wheel whose axle is not principal would need a cross-Jacobian effective mass, which is deliberately not provided.
- **Rejected approaches.** (1) Removing the axle component from `J_ang` of the single row: it also removed it from the wrench, so the solver moment disagreed with the geometric moment of the loads. (2) Full Jacobian plus a frozen bias `-(omega . u)(u . (r_i x n))` taken at prepare: the effective mass and every later iteration still used the full Jacobian, so an axle-spin change caused by the patch's own wrench fed back into `vn_i` (loads depended on the axial inertia); the bias is gone. (3) Projecting or diagonalising the wheel inertia inside the patch, or skipping the support of a wheel whose axle is not principal: either hides a body property from the user; the precondition is validated and reported instead. (4) A coupling tolerance proportional to the largest tensor element (`sqrt(J_xy^2 + J_xz^2) <= 1e-5 max|I_ab|`, the first version of the check): it is scale-independent but not anisotropy-independent, because for a small axial moment and large transverse moments it accepted a residual `rho` of 5e-3; replaced by the residual above (`test_o_high_anisotropy_*`). Tests: `test_n_*`, `test_o_*`.
- **Invalid derived coefficients.** `validate()` checks the parameters only; `gamma = 1/(dt (c_i + dt k_i))` also depends on the sample area and `dt`, so finite accepted parameters can still overflow (`k_A = 1e-305` with `c_A = 0` gives `gamma > 1.8e308`). A physically compressed sample (`g < 0`) whose `gamma`, `bias` or `split_bias` is not finite invalidates the whole patch for that step: all rows, carried impulses and load are cleared as for separation, `last_reason` names the coefficient and the sample, and the wheel receives nothing. A sample is never skipped as if it were uncompressed. No parameter bound was added: any fixed bound would be arbitrary without the area and `dt`.
- **Measured quadrature behaviour** (test tyre `W = 0.18`, `rho = 0.10`, `R = 0.31`, crown compression 2 cm, upright; error of `N` against a closed-form integral): 9x48 +1.3 %, 17x96 +0.2 %, 33x192 -0.06 %, 64x256 -0.014 % (cambered 0.15 rad: +2.3 %, -0.25 %, +0.04 %, +0.005 %). The sample count rises 136-fold (120 to 16384) while the load changes by less than 6 %; a fixed per-sample spring would rise with the count. The footprint boundary is a kink of `max(0, .)`, so the error is second order, the damping term (indicator of the footprint) first order. Samples rotate with the wheel, so for a fixed pose the load varies with the spin angle: spread 3.9 % (9x48), 0.10 % (17x96), 0.034 % (33x192).

## Tyre patch force integration (M2.5.3 Stage C)
The M2.4.2 tyre model ("Tyre behaviour" above: axes, slip, combined-slip law, rolling resistance, parameters and their meaning) driven by the AGGREGATE physical state of the Stage B patch. One tangential response and one rolling-resistance response per wheel; no generic friction row; no new tyre parameter. Implementation and ownership: `ARCHITECTURE.md`.
- **Aggregate state (recomputed from the current rows in every solver iteration, after the sequential normal rows of that iteration).** `Lambda_n = sum_i lambda_i` (N s, current accumulated physical normal impulses; never a cached, speculative, finish()-only or nominal value), `N = Lambda_n / dt`, effective point `p_eff = sum_i lambda_i p_i / Lambda_n` (`p_i` the Stage A sample positions of the start-of-step pose). Valid only for finite `Lambda_n > 0`; otherwise the no-load result (zero impulse, no point, no velocity, no tyre response; nothing is divided). The point moves with camber and with fore-aft or lateral load asymmetry because it follows the actual impulses.
- **Effective contact velocity.** `v_eff = v + omega x (p_eff - x)` from the current solver velocities. For a rigid wheel `v(p)` is affine in `p`, so this equals `sum_i lambda_i v(p_i) / Lambda_n` exactly: no per-sample velocity is evaluated. **Approximation:** one resultant tangential force acts at `p_eff`, driven by the load-weighted mean slip. The variation of the slip across the patch (spin slip from yaw and roll rates, different tread speeds of a cambered shoulder), a moment about the contact normal (self-aligning moment, turn slip) and a different slip for each sample are not modelled.
- **Frame and slip.** `TireModel.axes(u, n)` with the start-of-step axle `u`: `e_x = n x u / |n x u|`, `e_y = e_x x n`, orthonormal, in the ground plane, following yaw and camber; degenerate (`|n x u| < MIN_PLANE_PROJECTION`) means no tyre force while the normal support stays. Slip velocities `s_x = -(v_eff . e_x) + (R_eff - R) Omega`, `s_y = -(v_eff . e_y)`, `Omega = -(omega . u)` from the live velocities in each iteration; reference speed `D = sqrt(v_hub^2 + v_ref^2)` frozen at the start of the solve (before the normal warm start), as in the one-point contact.
- **Tangential response.** The M2.4.2 implicit relation `lambda = A sigma(lambda)`, `A = diag(phi k_x Lambda_n, phi k_y Lambda_n)`, `sigma = (s + G (lambda - lambda_cur)) / D`, solved as the closed-form 2x2 system (`phi` lagged one iteration) by the shared `TireModel.implicit_tangential_impulse`, with `G = S M^-1 P^T` built at the CURRENT `p_eff` (impulse rows: linear `e`, angular `r x e`, `r = p_eff - x`). The result is projected radially into the ONE ellipse `(lambda_x/(mu_x Lambda_n))^2 + (lambda_y/(mu_y Lambda_n))^2 <= 1`, `mu_x = grip_x mu_s`, `mu_y = grip_y mu_s`, with the aggregate `Lambda_n`: the sample count never multiplies the limit.
- **Impulse application.** The resultant `L = lambda_x e_x + lambda_y e_y` is applied to the wheel as the linear impulse `L` and the angular impulse `(p_eff - x) x L` (the physical wrench; the axle-projected velocity Jacobian of the normal rows is not used). Because `p_eff` changes while the normal rows converge, every iteration first re-seats the angular part to the current `p_eff` and then adds only the change, so the applied wrench is at all times the resultant force at the current effective point and no angular impulse of an earlier point remains. The ground is static and takes nothing.
- **Rolling resistance.** `M = c_rr N R_eff psi(R_eff Omega / v_rr)` about the axle with `N = Lambda_n/dt` of the current iteration and the frozen start-of-solve spin; `M dt` is applied as the difference to what this step already applied, once per wheel, outside the ellipse, never warm started, exactly zero without positive aggregate load. It is a moment about `+u` (against the spin of a forward-rolling wheel), not a longitudinal force (a force would be cancelled by the slip response, rejected in M2.4.2).
- **Warm start and lifecycle.** The converged tangential impulse is stored as a world vector, projected into the next tangent plane, scaled by `warm_start_factor`, clipped into the ellipse of the warm-started aggregate load and applied at the warm-started `p_eff`. With no positive aggregate normal impulse (separation, invalid sampling, abort, `reset_warm_start()`), disabled tyre forces or degenerate axes: tangential and rolling impulses, reported forces and the carried impulse are zero and no point or velocity survives; re-contact starts cold. A non-finite aggregate or derived solver value is a tyre failure: the slip, the response matrix, the tangential target, the rolling-resistance target, its change and the resulting angular velocity are each validated BEFORE anything reaches the wheel (the angular-velocity update is a float32 `Vector3`, so a finite float64 target can still overflow it). The tangential wrench is applied atomically: the scalar impulses and the contact point, the derived linear and angular impulses, their changes against the wrench already applied, the velocity changes and BOTH resulting velocities are all checked first, and only then are the two velocities and the record written; an invalid candidate changes neither (it is a failure, as above), so a recorded wrench is always finite and was fully applied. A failure withdraws everything the tyre response applied in that solve (tangential wrench and rolling moment, from their bookkeeping) and is TERMINAL for the rest of that solve: every later iteration returns before applying any tangential or rolling-resistance response, so no impulse can be applied after the failure and then hidden by clearing the record. The withdrawal is itself validate-then-write and atomic: the record, the wheel velocities, every velocity change and both resulting velocities are checked before either velocity is written, and the angular rollback reverses the order of the application (the rolling moment comes off first, then the tangential one) because float32 sums are not associative: removing them in the forward order can leave the rolling term as a residual spin. If the inverse is not representable in float32 (a finite wheel state near the float32 limit), nothing is clamped, written or cleared: the wheel keeps the recorded response, the record is HELD, the failure stays terminal, and a terminal numerical fault is reported (`last_reason`, and `validate()` so that `SimWorld` refuses to step) until `reset_warm_start()` after the wheel state is restored or repaired. `finish()` clears the whole patch (`last_reason`) and never discards the record of an impulse the wheel still carries; the next `prepare()` (or `reset_warm_start()` after an aborted step) starts a new valid solve cold. An invalid derived value is never clamped to a valid zero response (a valid zero, such as no load or zero spin, stays valid); samples are never skipped to keep an apparently valid aggregate. A tyre that fails validation makes the constraint invalid and `SimWorld` refuses to step.
- **Sample density.** Traction and rolling resistance depend on the aggregate load only, so refining the sampling changes them only through the normal-load quadrature error ("Tyre patch normal support", measured values there). A small slip is the difference of large terms (`v - Omega h`), so it also feels the sub-millimetre shift of the effective rolling height between resolutions (measured on the test tyre: 9x48 against 17x96 at spin 1.10 times the free-rolling spin, the small-slip force differs by a few percent while the saturated limit follows the aggregate load to 1e-4).
- **Rejected approaches.** (1) A second registered constraint owning the tyre: it could not see the current normal impulses in the same iteration without a solver change. (2) The tyre force in `finish()` or from the previous step's load: stale load, and an explicit force is unstable at these loads and the low axle inertia (M2.4.2). (3) One tyre response (or one friction ellipse) per sample: traction would grow with the sample count. (4) The tangential impulse at the wheel centre or at one sample: wrong moment (no camber or lever response). (5) Angular impulse accumulated at a moving point without re-seating: the moment of an earlier point stays in the wheel. (6) Reusing the normal rows' axle-projected velocity Jacobian for the tangential wrench: it removes the real axle moment. (7) Applying the rolling-resistance update without validating it, or letting a failed response resume in a later iteration of the same solve (the PR #23 review findings): the first writes a NaN angular velocity into the wheel before the failure is noticed, the second applies an impulse that `finish()` then erases from the record while the wheel keeps it. Clamping an invalid value to zero was rejected as well: it reports a failed response as a successful one. (8) Validating only the scalar impulses, or updating the linear velocity before the angular update is known to be finite (the PR #24 review finding): finite impulses and lever arms still overflow the float32 derived values, which would write INF or NaN into the wheel and leave the record half updated.

## Time
- Fixed timestep default 1/120 s, independent of render rate. Accumulator with frame-time clamp and step cap to avoid a spiral of death.
- Rendering interpolates between the previous and the published state: `alpha = accumulator / dt`.

## Numerical precision (Godot 4.7)
- `Vector3`, `Quaternion`, `Basis`, `Transform3D` are **single precision (float32)** in the standard Godot build; GDScript scalar `float` is 64-bit.
  Anything stored in a vector/basis/quaternion (positions, velocities, inertia, orientation) carries ~1e-7 relative error.
- Inertia validation thresholds above are chosen from this float32 epsilon (1.19e-7); tests use I·I⁻¹ ≈ identity at 1e-5.
- Test tolerances therefore use >= 1e-6 (relative to magnitude) for vector/basis quantities. Do NOT write 1e-9 tolerances on vector results.
  Tighter tolerances are legitimate only for float64 scalars (e.g. accumulator and simulation-time arithmetic) or exact discrete solutions computed in float64; see `TESTING.md`.
- Consequence for future work: long-running or large-coordinate scenes lose position resolution (float32 at 100 m ~ 8e-6 m).
  A double-precision Godot build (`precision=double`) is the migration path if this becomes limiting; record any such decision here.
- The gyroscopic solve is evaluated in float64 scalars; its inputs and its result pass through float32 `Vector3`/`Basis`, so `|L|` and `T` carry float32 round-off (random-walk, ~1e-6 relative over 10⁴ steps).
- Frame-clamp interplay: `max_frame_time / fixed_timestep` (0.1 s -> 12 steps) exceeds `max_steps_per_frame` (10), so the step cap is
  the binding limit for frames longer than ~0.083 s; the remaining backlog is dropped and reported in `dropped_time`.
